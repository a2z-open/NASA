## What was done

The search (`pipeline/hunt.py`) is given a patch of sky and its SPHEREx exposures, and nothing else. It stacks each
visit in three wavelength bands, detects every source, links detections across visits, measures every linked source
in every exposure on native pixels, and applies the same pre-declared motion test as the rest of PARALLAX
(≥ 5σ and |μ|/σ_μ ≥ 5). Only then are catalogues consulted.

## Limits

- The recovery fields were chosen because a known mover is there. They measure completeness, not a discovery rate.
- Motions are fitted without parallax when fewer than three visits are available; a nearby object's parallax then
  biases the linear motion (see WISE 0855).
- Stack-level candidates (too faint for single exposures) rest on two or three positions and are a weaker tier.
- Asteroids move out of a stack within hours and are rejected as outliers by design; this is not an asteroid search.
- A planet at several hundred au would move by arcminutes between visits (Earth's reflex motion), beyond these
  cutouts, and is expected to be too faint for one visit. This search targets nearby cold brown dwarfs and
  free-floating planets.
